            <h1>Free Energy Landscapes of a Membrane Transporter</h1>

            <p class="post-meta">June 15, 2026 · Tags: metadynamics, free energy, transporters</p>


            <h2>Motivation</h2>

            <p>

                Understanding how membrane transporters alternate between inward- and 

                outward-facing states is key to drug design. We used well-tempered 

                metadynamics to reconstruct the free energy surface along collective 

                variables describing the conformational change.

            </p>


            <h2>Simulation Setup</h2>

            <p>

                The system was built with CHARMM-GUI, embedded in a POPC bilayer, and 

                solvated with TIP3P water. All simulations ran with GROMACS 2023 

                patched with PLUMED.

            </p>


            <pre><code class="language-bash">

# Example PLUMED input

cv1: DISTANCE ATOMS=345,678

cv2: TORSION ATOMS=123,456,789,1011

metad: METAD ARG=cv1,cv2 SIGMA=0.1,0.2 HEIGHT=1.2 PACE=500 ...

            </code></pre>


            <h2>Results</h2>

            <p>

                The free energy surface revealed two major minima separated by a ~5 kcal/mol 

                barrier, consistent with experimental turnover rates. We identified a key 

                salt bridge that stabilizes the outward-facing state.

            </p>


            <h2>Takeaway</h2>

            <p>

                Metadynamics with carefully chosen CVs can map complex conformational 

                landscapes in membrane proteins. Next step: umbrella sampling validation.

            </p>
